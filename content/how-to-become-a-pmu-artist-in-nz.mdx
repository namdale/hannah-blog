Permanent makeup is one of the fastest-growing beauty careers in New Zealand. A skilled PMU artist works the hours they choose, builds a loyal client base, and earns premium rates for work that changes the way clients see themselves. But the path from complete beginner to fully booked artist has never been obvious. This guide covers what it actually takes.

## What does a PMU artist do?

Permanent makeup artists perform semi-permanent procedures such as nano hairstroke brows, lip blush, eyeliner tattoo and scalp micropigmentation (SMP). Each treatment implants pigment into the skin using a specialised machine and needles — far closer to tattoo artistry than to everyday makeup. That is exactly why proper training in skin anatomy, hygiene and colour theory is non-negotiable.

## Step 1 — Choose the right training

Not all courses are created equal. When you compare academies, look for:

- **One-on-one classes** — genuine hands-on training, not just theory slides on a screen
- **Live model work** under supervision before you graduate
- **Nano hairstroke technique** — finer and more natural than traditional microblading, and the current industry standard
- **Mentorship that continues after graduation** — your first year with real clients is when you need support the most

## Step 2 — Understand the regulations

In New Zealand, permanent makeup is classed as a skin-piercing service and falls under your local council's health regulations. You will need to operate from registered premises, follow strict sterilisation protocols, and in most councils hold an operator licence for the trade. A good academy will walk you through the compliance requirements for the area you plan to work in — make sure you ask before you enrol.

## Step 3 — Budget for your starter kit

Expect to invest in a quality PMU machine, needle cartridges, pigments, practice skins and hygiene supplies. Buying cheap equipment is the most common beginner mistake: an inconsistent machine produces inconsistent strokes, and clients can tell the difference.

## Step 4 — Build your portfolio and your Instagram

Your Instagram feed is your shop window. Photograph every healed result (with your client's permission), post consistently, and document your learning journey — clients genuinely enjoy watching an artist grow. Above all, before-and-after content of healed work builds more trust than anything else you can post.

## When does the income start?

Most graduates take their first paying clients within a few weeks of finishing their training — starting at model rates, then raising prices as the portfolio grows. But here is the part that matters most: keep practising, consistently. Skill comes first, and good income follows skill naturally.
